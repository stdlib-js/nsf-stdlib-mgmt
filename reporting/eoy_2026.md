## Accomplishments

### What are the major goals of the project?

The goal of this project is to establish a sustainable open source ecosystem for scientific computing on the web centered around stdlib, a foundational library for numerical and scientific computation in JavaScript and WebAssembly.

The project seeks to reduce fragmentation within the JavaScript scientific computing ecosystem by promoting shared infrastructure, common data structures, interoperability, and community-driven standards. Activities are organized around three primary objectives:

1. Community building and contributor experience, including governance, onboarding, mentoring, outreach, and contributor growth.

2. Infrastructure development, including documentation, automation, continuous integration, monitoring, quality assurance, and release processes needed to support long-term ecosystem sustainability.

3. Adoption and standardization, including collaboration with existing numerical computing projects to encourage interoperability, shared infrastructure, and coordinated ecosystem growth.

Together, these efforts are intended to establish the organizational, technical, and social foundations necessary for a sustainable scientific computing ecosystem for web-native technologies.




### What was accomplished under these goals (you must provide information for at least one of the 4 categories below)?

#### Major Activities

During the first year of the project, activities focused on establishing the organizational, community, and technical foundations necessary to support a sustainable open source ecosystem for scientific computing on the web.

On the community side, the project formalized its governance structure, established recurring weekly office hours, expanded contributor engagement efforts, migrated community communications to Zulip, published ecosystem-focused blog content, and continued mentoring contributors through programs such as Google Summer of Code (GSoC). The project also completed the NSF I-Corps training program and conducted more than 100 ecosystem interviews to better understand community needs, barriers to adoption, and opportunities for ecosystem growth.

On the infrastructure side, the project conducted a comprehensive review of contributor onboarding processes, developed contributor user stories, improved internal tooling documentation, modernized project documentation and examples, strengthened continuous integration and security workflows, expanded automated quality assurance processes, and continued development of infrastructure supporting versioned documentation, analytics, dashboards, and release automation.

On the adoption and standardization side, the project engaged maintainers of several numerical computing projects within the JavaScript ecosystem, including scijs, jStat, Math.js, and FEAScript, to explore opportunities for interoperability, shared infrastructure, and adoption of stdlib technologies.

#### Specific Objectives

Significant progress was made toward the project's stated objectives.

The project completed a formal governance document defining project leadership pathways, responsibilities, and decision-making processes. Weekly public office hours were established and maintained throughout the reporting period, providing an ongoing forum for mentoring, onboarding, technical discussion, and community coordination.

The project conducted an audit of contributor onboarding materials and processes and developed contributor user stories to identify barriers to participation and improve the experience of new contributors. Internal tooling documentation was expanded, contributor training materials were developed, and an initial library of instructional screencasts was created.

Infrastructure improvements included strengthening security practices through workflow audits and token rotation procedures, expanding automated code review processes using AI-assisted tooling, improving static analysis workflows, automating changelog generation across standalone repositories, and maintaining automated code coverage reporting for all commits and pull requests.

The project also initiated collaboration activities with ecosystem partners and developed proof-of-concept integrations demonstrating how stdlib infrastructure can be leveraged by downstream projects.


#### Significant Results

The project experienced substantial community growth during the reporting period.

Since the start of the award, 167 contributors have participated in the project, including 146 new contributors who successfully landed at least one contribution. The overall contributor community grew to 363 contributors. Weekly office hours attracted approximately 10-20 participants per session and provided an ongoing mechanism for contributor engagement, onboarding, mentoring, and coordination.

The project continued to increase its public visibility. The main GitHub repository reached 5,854 stars during the reporting period. The project also expanded its technical footprint, reaching 5,555 published npm packages, with additional packages awaiting publication.

The project successfully completed its migration to Zulip, providing a more scalable platform for community communication, mentoring, and technical collaboration.

The project was selected for participation in Google Summer of Code for the third consecutive year and is currently supporting five GSoC contributors working on foundational scientific computing technologies. Eight mentors are participating in supporting contributor development through the program.

Documentation, onboarding resources, governance processes, security practices, and project infrastructure were significantly strengthened during the reporting period. The project also completed NSF I-Corps training activities and conducted more than 100 stakeholder interviews, generating valuable information about ecosystem needs, adoption barriers, and opportunities for long-term sustainability.


#### Key outcomes or other achievements

A key outcome of the first year was the establishment of foundational ecosystem structures necessary for long-term sustainability.

The project successfully transitioned from an effort primarily focused on software development to one increasingly focused on ecosystem development. Governance processes were formalized, recurring community engagement mechanisms were established, contributor growth accelerated, and collaboration with downstream projects expanded.

The project strengthened its capacity to recruit, onboard, mentor, and retain contributors through improved documentation, mentoring practices, communication infrastructure, and community processes. These investments have helped create a more accessible pathway for new contributors to participate in scientific open source software development.

The project also strengthened relationships with other organizations and open source projects working in scientific computing. Discussions, proof-of-concept integrations, and collaboration planning with projects such as scijs, jStat, Math.js, and FEAScript have begun laying the groundwork for future interoperability and standardization efforts.

Finally, the project increased awareness of scientific computing on the web through public communications, blog posts, conference presentations by community members, and outreach activities. These efforts have helped broaden understanding of the role web-native technologies can play in scientific computing, data analysis, machine learning, and research software development.



### What opportunities for training and professional development has the project provided?

The project has provided numerous opportunities for training, mentoring, and professional development for contributors ranging from first-time open source participants to experienced software developers.

A primary mechanism for contributor development has been the project's weekly public office hours, which have been held continuously throughout the reporting period. These meetings provide opportunities for technical discussion, project coordination, onboarding, mentorship, and community engagement. Office hours typically attract between 10 and 20 participants and serve as an important venue for knowledge sharing and contributor support.

The project has also continued its participation in Google Summer of Code (GSoC), through which contributors receive structured mentorship while working on substantial scientific computing and software infrastructure projects. During the reporting period, the project supported five GSoC contributors and involved eight mentors who provided technical guidance, code review, project planning assistance, and career development support.

Beyond formal programs, contributors receive ongoing mentorship through daily interactions on the project's communication platforms, weekly office hours, code review processes, and one-on-one discussions with project leaders and experienced contributors. These activities help contributors develop skills in software engineering, scientific computing, open source collaboration, documentation, testing, and community leadership.

The project also invested in improving contributor onboarding and training resources. Activities included auditing existing onboarding processes, developing contributor user stories, expanding internal tooling documentation, creating training materials, and developing an initial set of instructional screencasts designed to help new contributors become productive more quickly.

The project team participated in the NSF I-Corps training program during the reporting period. As part of this effort, team members conducted more than 100 interviews with users, contributors, and stakeholders throughout the ecosystem. This experience provided valuable professional development in customer discovery, stakeholder engagement, ecosystem analysis, and sustainability planning.

In addition, members of the broader stdlib community participated in conference presentations and outreach activities that helped develop communication, teaching, and technical leadership skills. The project also supported community members participating in ongoing research studies examining the impact of AI-assisted software development, providing contributors with opportunities to engage with emerging software engineering research and evaluation methodologies.

Collectively, these activities have helped contributors develop technical expertise, open source leadership experience, communication skills, mentoring experience, and a deeper understanding of how sustainable scientific software ecosystems are built and maintained.



### How have the results been disseminated to communities of interest?

Project results have been disseminated through public communications, open development practices, recurring community events, conference presentations, mentoring programs, and direct engagement with ecosystem stakeholders.

The project maintains a public blog used to communicate project developments, community initiatives, ecosystem observations, and opportunities for participation. During the reporting period, the project published posts announcing the migration to Zulip, introducing the project's guiding principles through the "Zen of stdlib," announcing Google Summer of Code participants and projects, and discussing the impact of AI on open source communities and contributor engagement.

The project also disseminates results through weekly public office hours, which have been held continuously throughout the reporting period. These meetings provide an open forum for contributors, users, and ecosystem partners to learn about project developments, ask questions, discuss technical topics, and participate in project coordination.

The project continues to operate using an open development model. Governance documents, issue tracking, pull requests, technical discussions, development activity, and community planning occur publicly through GitHub and community communication channels. The migration to Zulip further strengthened the project's ability to support public discussion, contributor onboarding, and technical collaboration.

Members of the broader stdlib community also disseminated project ideas through conference presentations. Presentations during the reporting period included talks at DevConf.IN 2026 and the HPSF Conference 2026 focused on scientific computing, open source software, and the role of JavaScript in computational applications.

The project has also reached communities of interest through Google Summer of Code, where contributors work on public scientific computing infrastructure and share progress through public repositories, documentation, and community discussions.

The project team completed the NSF I-Corps training program and conducted more than 100 interviews with stakeholders across the scientific computing, open source, and web development communities. These interviews both informed project strategy and increased awareness of the project's goals, ecosystem vision, and opportunities for collaboration.

Finally, the project has engaged maintainers and contributors from related open source projects, including scijs, jStat, Math.js, and FEAScript, to disseminate ideas related to interoperability, standardization, and shared infrastructure for scientific computing on the web.


### What do you plan to do during the next reporting period to accomplish the goals?

During the next reporting period, the project will focus on converting the foundational work completed during Year 1 into more mature ecosystem infrastructure, broader adoption, and more systematic community support.

On the community side, the project will continue weekly public office hours, daily contributor support through Zulip, and structured mentorship through Google Summer of Code and related contributor activities. The project will also continue improving contributor onboarding by publishing tailored onboarding documentation, polishing and releasing the initial library of contributor screencasts, expanding internal tooling documentation, and developing additional tutorials and examples for scientific computing with stdlib. The project also plans to publish additional blog posts and other public-facing materials to increase awareness of stdlib and scientific computing on the web.

On the infrastructure side, the project will prioritize work that was delayed by staffing turnover during the first reporting period. Planned activities include completing the deployment workflow for versioned documentation, advancing release automation, continuing work on nightly builds, improving website analytics and project dashboards, expanding automated quality assurance workflows, and beginning work on canary testing infrastructure for downstream dependencies. The project also plans to continue strengthening CI, security, code coverage, changelog generation, and automated maintenance workflows so that the ecosystem can scale to support more contributors and downstream users.

On the adoption and standardization side, the project will continue collaboration with scijs, jStat, Math.js, FEAScript, and other downstream projects. The emphasis will be on moving from proof-of-concept integrations toward more concrete adoption plans, identifying the technical and social barriers to integration, and prioritizing collaborations where downstream maintainers have near-term availability. For scijs and jStat, the project plans to further refine existing proof-of-concept work. For Math.js, the project will continue coordination discussions and evaluate practical integration mechanisms. For FEAScript and other emerging downstream users, the project will provide technical support and use their adoption experience to inform documentation, interoperability, and API design priorities.

The project also plans to use the results of the NSF I-Corps stakeholder interviews to guide Year 2 priorities. In particular, the project will synthesize lessons from the interviews to better understand user needs, contributor motivations, adoption barriers, and potential sustainability pathways. These findings will inform community programming, documentation priorities, partnership development, and future outreach.

Finally, the project plans to address the current staffing gap by hiring a replacement senior engineer and an additional senior engineer during the next one to two months. These hires are expected to increase project capacity and accelerate progress on infrastructure-related objectives during the next reporting period.

The overall goals of the project remain unchanged: to build a sustainable open source ecosystem for scientific computing on the web by strengthening community capacity, improving technical infrastructure, and promoting interoperability and shared standards across the JavaScript scientific computing ecosystem.

### What is the impact on the development of the principal discipline(s) of the project? 

The project is contributing to the development of scientific computing on the web by strengthening the organizational, technical, and community foundations needed to support a sustainable ecosystem for numerical computation in JavaScript and WebAssembly.

These activities help reduce fragmentation within the JavaScript scientific computing ecosystem and create conditions for greater interoperability, standardization, and reuse of shared infrastructure.

The project is also helping establish scientific computing as a first-class use case for web-native technologies. Through community engagement, technical development, and collaboration with downstream projects, the project is advancing the broader goal of making the web a viable platform for data analysis, numerical computing, machine learning, and scientific research applications.


### What is the impact on other disciplines?

The project's emphasis on web-native scientific computing has potential relevance across a wide range of disciplines that increasingly rely on computational tools, including data science, engineering, education, life sciences, social sciences, and machine learning.

By lowering barriers to deploying computational tools through web browsers and other web-enabled environments, the project helps make scientific software more accessible to researchers, educators, students, and practitioners who may not have access to specialized computing environments.

The project's work on interoperability and shared infrastructure also has implications beyond scientific computing itself, providing lessons for ecosystem development, open source governance, contributor experience, and standards adoption that may be applicable in other software communities.
